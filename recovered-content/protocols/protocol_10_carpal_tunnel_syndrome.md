# Carpal Tunnel Syndrome — Treatment Protocol
_Median Nerve Compression at the Wrist_

## Summary
Compression of the median nerve within the carpal tunnel, raising intra-tunnel pressure and impairing conduction. Conservative management reduces pressure, restores nerve excursion and offloads the wrist; refer if severe.

**Pathology.** Carpal tunnel syndrome is compression of the median nerve within the carpal tunnel (bounded by the carpal bones and the flexor retinaculum), raising intra-tunnel pressure and impairing nerve conduction. Conservative management aims to reduce pressure, restore nerve excursion and offload the wrist; severe cases with thenar wasting may need surgical referral.

## Clinical presentation (signs & symptoms)
Median-distribution paraesthesia (thumb, index, middle, radial ring), nocturnal symptoms, flick-sign relief, hand weakness/clumsiness; thenar atrophy in chronic cases.

## Assessment (Examination & Assessment)
### Subjective
Numbness/tingling in the median distribution (thumb, index, middle, radial half of ring finger); worse at night; shaking the hand relieves it ("flick sign"); weakness/clumsiness, dropping objects; aggravated by gripping/sustained wrist positions. Pain scale.

### Objective
Phalen's test; reverse Phalen's; Tinel's sign over the carpal tunnel; median nerve provocation (ULNT median); thenar wasting (chronic); grip/pinch strength; sensation testing in median distribution.

### Differentiate from
Cervical radiculopathy (C6/C7), pronator teres syndrome, thoracic outlet syndrome.

### Key special tests
| Test | What a positive result indicates |
|---|---|
| Phalen's test | sustained wrist flexion provokes median paraesthesia |
| Tinel's sign over the carpal tunnel | tapping provokes paraesthesia |
| Median upper-limb neurodynamic test (ULNT) | neural provocation |
| Thenar strength / wasting (APB) | severity marker |
| Sensation testing (median distribution) | maps sensory loss |

## Red flags & when to refer
- Thenar wasting or motor deficit — surgical referral (carpal tunnel release)
- Cervical radiculopathy (C6/C7) or double-crush
- Acute, severe or rapidly progressive deficit
- Systemic contributors (hypothyroidism, rheumatoid arthritis, pregnancy, diabetes)

## Contraindications & precautions
- Do NOT dry needle within the carpal tunnel or over the median nerve; needle forearm musculature only, with care.
- Neural mobilisation must be gentle and non-provocative — aggressive tensioning worsens nerve irritation.
- Rule out cervical radiculopathy / double-crush; refer for surgical opinion if thenar wasting or severe/persistent deficit.

## Treatment protocol
### Session structure & time scales
- Pre-assessment + prep (10 min): Phalen's, Tinel's, sensation, strength.
- Soft tissue — forearm flexors & pronators (12–15 min).
- Wrist/carpal mobilisations (5 min).
- Median nerve gliding / neural mobilisation — gentle (6–8 min).
- Electrotherapy — US to forearm (not over the tunnel) (8 min).
- Splint & ergonomic advice + nerve-glide demo (5–8 min).
- Reassessment + home programme (5 min).

### Modalities & rationale
| Modality / technique | Rationale (why it is used for this pathology) |
|---|---|
| Soft tissue (forearm flexors/pronators) | reduces flexor tone that can raise carpal-tunnel pressure. |
| Wrist/carpal mobilisations | improve carpal mobility and reduce mechanical irritation (Banks, 2013). |
| Median nerve gliding (neural mobilisation) | restores nerve excursion — gentle, non-provocative, within tolerance. |
| Therapeutic ultrasound (forearm) | parameter-dependent support (Watson, 2008); not directly over the nerve/tunnel. |
| Splinting & ergonomics | neutral-wrist night splint and activity modification — key conservative management. |

## Loading & exercise progression
Not a loading condition. Median nerve gliding (gentle, non-provocative), forearm mobility, neutral-wrist night splint, ergonomic modification. Refer if severe or thenar wasting.

## Home care / self-management
Neutral-wrist night splint, median nerve glides, forearm-flexor stretches, ergonomic modification (workstation, tool use), activity pacing, avoid sustained end-range wrist positions.

## Outcome measures & re-assessment
- Pain scale (NPRS 0–10) & paraesthesia frequency pre/post and over sessions.
- Repeat Phalen's/Tinel's.
- Reassess grip/pinch strength & sensation.
- Night-symptom trend.

## Anatomy reference
| Structure | Origin | Insertion | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carpal tunnel | floor: carpal bones; roof: flexor retinaculum (transverse carpal ligament) | flexor retinaculum: scaphoid tubercle & trapezium (radial) to pisiform & hook of hamate (ulnar) | contains median nerve + 9 flexor tendons (FDS×4, FDP×4, FPL) |
| Flexor digitorum superficialis | medial epicondyle (common flexor), ulna, radius | middle phalanges of digits 2–5 | flexes PIP joints & wrist |
| Abductor pollicis brevis (thenar) | flexor retinaculum & scaphoid | proximal phalanx of thumb | abducts thumb (median-innervated) |
| Opponens pollicis (thenar) | flexor retinaculum & trapezium | 1st metacarpal | opposes thumb (median-innervated) |

## Clinical reasoning (Q&A for the system)
_These question–answer pairs encode the expected clinical reasoning for this condition. Use them to justify decisions and to check generated plans._

**Q: What is the sensory distribution of the median nerve in the hand?**
A: The thumb, index, middle finger and the radial half of the ring finger.

**Q: Which tests would you use for carpal tunnel syndrome?**
A: Phalen's and Tinel's over the carpal tunnel and the median ULNT; the 'flick sign' (shaking relieves it) is a useful historical feature.

**Q: Describe the boundaries and contents of the carpal tunnel.**
A: Floor: the carpal bones; roof: the flexor retinaculum (scaphoid tubercle/trapezium to pisiform/hook of hamate). It contains the median nerve and nine flexor tendons (FDS×4, FDP×4, FPL).

**Q: Why would you never dry needle inside the tunnel?**
A: The median nerve runs through it — needling risks nerve injury; needle the forearm musculature only, with care.

**Q: When would you refer for a surgical opinion, and what differentials apply?**
A: Refer with thenar wasting or a severe/persistent deficit (may need carpal tunnel release). Differentials: cervical radiculopathy (C6/7), pronator teres syndrome and thoracic outlet syndrome.
